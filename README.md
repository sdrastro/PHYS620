# PHYS620

Computer-based homework assignments and Jupyter notebook tutorials for PHYS 620, Classical Mechanics

# List of concepts we have covered

## Calculus of variations

- Physical motivations for reformulating Newtonian mechanics
- Definition of variational principle
- Fermat's least time principle
- Definition of functional
- Derivation of Euler-Lagrange equations
  - Perturbation function $\eta$ and dimensionless factor $\alpha$
  - Writing $dS/d\alpha$ as a limit as $\alpha \rightarrow 0$
  - Role of directional derivative
- Shortest path between two points
- Brachistochrone problem
- Writing Euler-Lagrange equations for multivariable problems
- Relationship between dimensionality $d$, number of particles
  $N$, and total number of generalized coordinates $n$

## Lagrangian mechanics

- Action integral / Hamilton's principle / principle of least
  action
- Qualitatively, why Lagrangian is $T - U$ instead of $T + U$
  (illustrate with ball drop)
- How to recover Newton's 2nd law from the Euler-Lagrange
  equation for a free particle
- Deriving Kepler's 2nd law for a planet orbiting a star
- Defining generalized coordinates, generalized force, and
  generalized momentum for constrained systems 
- Proof that the Euler-Lagrange equation is valid for any
  holonomic system and that constraint forces can be neglected
- Hamiltonian and conservation of energy
- Generalized Lagrangian with E&M example

In general you will not have to replicate an entire derivation or
proof on a test - in particular, the derivation of the
Euler-Lagrange equation and the proof that it is valid for any
holonomic system are beyond what you could explain in a test
period. But if I give you one or two steps, you should be able
to explain them, or fill in a missing step.
